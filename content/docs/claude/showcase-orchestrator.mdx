<InOneMinute>

- This is the top of the tree in the [Skills Showcase](/docs/claude/skills-showcase). Nothing invokes it, it invokes everything else.
- Its job is routing, not doing. The body is a phase list with instructions for which skill to reach for at each one.
- Its `description` has to cover the whole process in one or two sentences, specific enough that Claude reaches for it, and not a phase skill directly, when the request is genuinely "build me something new."
- Two of its eight phases, research and planning, fan out into their own sub-skills. The other six stay flat.

</InOneMinute>

## Why the description matters most here

An orchestrator's description has to do two jobs a phase skill's does not. It has to be broad enough to match "I want to build X" before any phase has even been chosen, and specific enough that Claude does not reach for it once the user is already deep in one phase. "Review my competitor list" should match [Competitor research](/docs/claude/showcase-competitors-research) directly, not restart the whole process from the top.

That is a harder needle to thread than it looks. A description this broad, "runs the full process for building a new digital product", would be a bad description for any phase skill: too vague to tell Claude when to prefer it over a sibling. It works here only because nothing else in the tree is competing for the same, very general request.

## Where it fits

Nothing sits above this skill. It is the entry point for the whole example. It hands off to eight phase skills, in a fixed order: [Understanding the idea](/docs/claude/showcase-understanding-of-idea), [Research](/docs/claude/showcase-research), [Product definition](/docs/claude/showcase-product-definition), [Planning](/docs/claude/showcase-planning), [Implementation](/docs/claude/showcase-implementation), [Validation and testing](/docs/claude/showcase-validation-testing), [SEO](/docs/claude/showcase-seo), and [Accessibility](/docs/claude/showcase-accessibility).
