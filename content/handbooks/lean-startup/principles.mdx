<InOneMinute>

- Five principles sit under everything else. Skip them and the tools become theatre.
- Two are about who does this work. Three are about how progress gets measured.
- The hardest one is validated learning, because it means treating your plan as a set of guesses.

</InOneMinute>

## The five

| Principle | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Entrepreneurs are everywhere** | This is a skill set, not a personality. It can be learned, and it applies inside big companies too |
| **Entrepreneurship is management** | A startup needs managing, not just building. The scarce skill is deciding what not to build |
| **Validated learning** | Progress is what you have proved with real customers, not what you have shipped |
| **Innovation accounting** | Boring but necessary: measure progress, set learning milestones, hold people to them |
| **Build, measure, learn** | The loop. Turn ideas into products, measure the response, learn whether to pivot or keep going |

## 1. Entrepreneurs are everywhere

You do not need a garage or a funding round. Anyone working on something new under real uncertainty is doing this job, including a team of three inside a large company.

The practical consequence: these methods are learnable. Nobody has to be born with it.

## 2. Entrepreneurship is management

The instinct in a new product is to focus on the technical work. Ries argues the opposite. The building is rarely the constraint. Deciding what to build, and having the discipline to build only that, is the constraint.

A startup is an institution, so it needs management. Just not the kind that produces a two-year roadmap.

## 3. Validated learning

Every startup plan rests on guesses. Validated learning means turning those guesses into experiments and letting real customer behavior settle them.

The test is simple: what do you now know that you did not know before, and what evidence backs it? "We shipped four features" is not an answer.

<Callout type="warn" title="Learning is not a consolation prize">
It is easy to call a failed release "a learning experience" afterward. That is not validated learning. Validated learning means you decided in advance what the experiment would prove, and it proved it.
</Callout>

## 4. Innovation accounting

You cannot use normal financial measures on a product with no revenue yet, so a startup needs its own way to show progress.

That means setting learning milestones, checking real numbers against them, and holding the team accountable to those numbers. [Innovation accounting](/handbooks/lean-startup/innovation-accounting) covers how.

## 5. Build, measure, learn

The loop that connects the other four. Build the smallest thing that tests a guess, measure what people do with it, learn whether the guess held, and decide.

Each pass should end with a decision, not just a release.

<CheckItWorked>
Ask your team what they learned in the last month that changed a decision. If the answers are all features and dates, you are running a delivery process, not a discovery one.
</CheckItWorked>
